What this view shows

  • Measured: probe path and field vector (Jupiter-Sun-Ecliptic frame, Sun toward +X, +Z up; Jupiter is drawn spinning about Z), plotted as 4-minute medians of the survey files. Br is from the System III file. The Br colouring only separates positive from negative; values under 0.5 nT in size are grey.
  • Why some gaps exist is not in the data files. Two published accounts: the 1998-02-10 flyby (E13) entry of the Galileo timeline says no remote sensing or magnetospheric data were collected because of solar conjunction, which reduced the capacity to transmit science data to Earth; the entry describes that flyby only, and it is shown wherever there is no field data between 1997-12-20 and 1998-03-28 (orbit 13 has magnetometer points inside that span). A Spaceflight Now report says the spacecraft entered safe mode at 13:41 UTC on 2002-01-17, 28 minutes before closest approach to Io, and the instruments went to standby; it is shown for the field-data gap that contains that time (2002-01-17 12:46 to 2002-01-18 00:22 UTC).
  • Gaps in the coloured path or the strips are gaps in the archive's field and instrument files. The light-grey trace is the whole path from the archive's trajectory files, drawn up to the current time and fading with age like the coloured paths. Where the grey dot or trace has no colour over it, the position comes from the trajectory file and nothing was measured there by the magnetometer. Where the trajectory files have a hole, positions from the field files are used if they exist. A few stretches have no position in the archive at all (the longest are about 40 and 42 days, January to March 1998); the dot is hidden there.
  • Readout symbols: the atlas carries values on the field file's own time grid. Status: ● the cursor is at a point of that grid; ○ no point of that grid here (position only); ∅ no position in the archive. Instrument letters: ● a value at this grid point (a coded zero counts as a value); ○ no value at this grid point; ∅ no values for that instrument anywhere in this segment's points. These describe what the atlas carries, not what each instrument's own files contain. Each small square under "layers" is one ticked layer, filled when it has a value here.
  • Run: playback goes through every orbit in time order, loading the next one as it goes, and runs through the gaps between orbit files at the real date (use a faster speed to cross the long ones). Choose "This orbit" to stay inside one. Earlier orbits stay drawn and fade with age (the oldest fade to a fixed, faint minimum).
  • Graph: the main panel shows whatever the path colour is set to (field strength, signed Br, or any instrument layer) as a per-pixel mean with its minimum-to-maximum range shaded. The panels can be resized: drag the bars between them, or use the Layout buttons.
  • Other instruments (Data types): each value is the time-weighted mean of that instrument's own samples overlapping a 4-minute point. Nothing is interpolated across gaps, and a point needs at least a quarter of its 4 minutes covered. Values are drawn on a log scale; each layer's range runs from the 0.2nd to the 99.9th percentile of its non-zero values over the mission, rounded outward to the next 0.1 in log10, and larger values are drawn at the top colour. Values are as given in the PDS files: this page applies no calibration, background correction or unit conversion.
  • What the layers are: EPD, 11-minute scan-average count rates from the archive's "C_" files (adjusted by the archive for gaps between detector cycles), averaged over the 16 sectors. PLS, spin-averaged count rates summed over the energy bands present in each record (14 at most; some records lack band 1 or 2 and are summed over the rest). PWS, geometric mean of the 1-minute spectral density over the frequency range shown. HIC, low-resolution survey count rates. DDS, dust-detector events per hour in a 1-hour window; a zero can mean no events or no operation, and no operating mask is applied.
  • Coverage: EPD channels A3 to A5, TP1, TO1 and TS1 have values only in orbits 3, 7 to 11, 16, 17 and 24 (A3 also 18). PWS magnetic spectra end in March 2003. HIC samples are sparse away from the moon passes.
  • Moons: positions come from the PDS moon-trajectory tables, which cover a few days around each orbit (solid bar on the time strip). Between tables each moon's position is interpolated from its own table values and an eccentric-orbit fit to them (dotted). The planet is drawn at a constant rotation rate within each survey file; against the System III longitudes stored in the files the drawn rotation differs by at most 1.4 degrees. The probe position is interpolated linearly between 4-minute points and is hidden where the gap to the nearest point is longer than the gap limit. Against the closest-approach distances in the PDS flyby tables, the probe-to-moon distance in this view differs by a median of 244 km over the 34 Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto passes (22 within 1,000 km, 27 within 2,000 km). The largest differences are E16 at 594,916 km, E18 at 254,192 km and I33 at 54,693 km, where the survey file has no point within hours of closest approach, then I25 at 7,695 km and C30 at 6,786 km.
  • File overlaps: at 11 of the 38 file boundaries the last 4-minute point of one file is later than the first point of the next (by up to 2,791 s, at ORB23 to ORB24). At those boundaries the clock steps back by the overlap and the overlapping span appears twice.
  • Not included: radio-occultation profiles (RSS), star-scanner records (SSD), the high-resolution moon-pass products of EPD, PLS, PWS and HIC, and any quantity away from the path. Apart from the moon interpolation and planet rotation phase described above, nothing here is fitted or predicted.
  • Illustration: the optional dipole lines only show the shape of a tilted dipole. Strength is not drawn and it is not a fit.

What this view shows

  • Measured: field vector in the moon's own frame, and the probe's path relative to the moon (PDS data). High-resolution files are shown as 3-second medians.
  • Axes: x along the direction the plasma corotates with Jupiter, y toward Jupiter, z north.
  • Survey-only passes: where the archive has no high-resolution file, the path comes from the PDS flyby table and the field from the survey file (samples every few tens of seconds to minutes), rotated into the moon's frame. These can miss fast changes.
  • Other instruments: drawn from the same survey products, on a 1-minute grid. A bin shows the instrument samples that overlap it, so a slow instrument repeats across several bins and a fast change can be missed. The high-resolution moon-pass products of these instruments are not included.
  • Not included: any quantity away from the path.
  • Illustration: the optional Ganymede dipole lines only show the shape of a dipole. Strength and tilt are not drawn and nothing is fitted.
Data: NASA PDS Planetary Plasma Interactions node. Other instruments: EPD (GO-J-EPD-2-REDR-RTS-SCAN-AVG-V1.0), PLS (GO-J-PLS-4-SUMM-BROWSE-V1.0), PWS (GO-J-PWS-4-SUMM-SA60S-V1.0), HIC (GO-J-HIC-3-RDR-SURVEY-COUNTRATE-V1.0), DDS (GO-D-GDDS-5-DUST-V4.1). Magnetometer: Galileo magnetospheric survey (GO-J-MAG-3-RDR-MAGSPHERIC-SURVEY-V1.0) and moon-flyby high-resolution sets (GO-J-MAG-3-HIGHRES-V1.0), spacecraft trajectories relative to the moons (GO-J-POS-6-SC-TRAJ-MOON-COORDS-V1.0) and moon trajectories (GO-J-POS-6-MOON-TRAJ-JUP-CRDS-V1.0). Moon sizes: mean radii. Dipole tilt used for the illustration: about 9.6° (a drawing parameter, not measured here). Planet and moon surfaces are drawn, not imaged.
